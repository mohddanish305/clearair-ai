    Method & sources
    
    
    What the numbers are, what they are not, and where they come from.
    
    
      
      
    • Data: Open-Meteo Air Quality API (Copernicus CAMS), free, no key. CAMS is a modelled grid (~11–45 km) — an estimate for your area, not a CPCB ground-station reading; a nearby station can differ.
      • 
      
    • India CPCB NAQI is computed here from pollutant sub-indices using CPCB breakpoints, on the trailing 24-hour mean — how CPCB defines the daily index. US EPA AQI comes from the same live feed. The scales genuinely differ, which is why both are shown.
      • 
      
    • WHO comparison: PM2.5 against the WHO 2021 24-hour guideline of 15 µg/m³.
      • 
      
    • Cigarette equivalent uses the Berkeley Earth rule of thumb: a 24-hour average of 22 µg/m³ PM2.5 ≈ one cigarette a day. An illustration of dose, not a medical measure.
      • 
      
    • Safety logic is deterministic. Verdicts, risk scores and the severe-day protocol come from fixed rules over the live data. AI text only phrases and personalises — it never sets a safety verdict.
      • 
      
    • Impact figures (CO₂ saved, exposure avoided) are estimates from your answers and standard per-trip factors, labelled as estimates.
      • 
    
    
    
    Sources: Open-Meteo / CAMS air quality · CPCB (India NAQI) · WHO Global Air Quality Guidelines 2021 · Berkeley Earth — cigarette equivalence
    
    
    Pollen data is not shown for India: the live pollen feed covers Europe only, and showing invented pollen numbers would be worse than showing none.
